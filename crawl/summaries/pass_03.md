## 4. Observations

- **The vocabulary has no terms for RNA-binding and chromatin work at all.** This batch added
  biotinylated RNA pull-down (4 papers, 2 targets), RIP, eCLIP, ChIP-qPCR and the pre-mRNA splicing
  function (3 papers). Together these are the standard assays of an RNA-binding protein or a
  chromatin factor, and none of them can be labelled today. This is the same kind of gap phase
  separation showed in pass 2, but larger: it covers a whole protein class, not one behaviour.
  Terms T028-T031 and F003 belong in one proposal.
- **Enzyme function terms stop at "Enzyme activity".** OGT's O-GlcNAc transferase activity has no
  term (3 papers), and FADS2's desaturase index has none either. The term table has specific
  function terms for kinases, proteases, phosphatases and several histone enzymes, but a
  glycosyltransferase or a desaturase falls back to the generic value, so the campaigns cannot be
  distinguished. Proposing terms one enzyme family at a time will not close this; the family list
  itself is the gap.
- **The cellular-activity link ENZ3 is again the most common new link** (AKR1C1, AOC3, FADS2, OGT):
  four of the five metabolic enzymes in this batch have a cellular readout of their own product
  that the database does not connect to them. This is now the pattern across all three passes.
- **Target engagement has outgrown CETSA.** Two proteome-wide or dose-resolved variants appeared in
  one AKR1C1 paper alone (PISA, ITDRF), and CETSA itself was a new readout for four targets
  (ADORA1, AFF1, AKR1C1, IGFBP6). The single CETSA term now covers three different experiments.
- **Name collisions now cost 36 papers across the crawl, and this batch found the two worst cases
  yet.** All 10 RPS15 papers were RIG-I studies, matched through the synonym "RIG"; RPS15 itself
  is never assayed in any of them. Four of AFF1's ten were matched through "AF4", which is also
  the abbreviation for asymmetrical flow field-flow fractionation. Both are short uppercase
  synonyms, the same shape as UNC119's "RG4" in pass 2.
  - The measured share now has **two** targets at 0.92, UNC119 and RPS15, and both are real
    collisions that cost the whole target. A guard on that extreme alone would have saved 15
    papers over three batches and has yet to produce a false positive.
  - The middle of the range is still undecidable from the number: AFF1 at 0.34 is a true collision,
    GPBAR1 at 0.48 is a legitimate common name. So the extreme-share guard is safe to apply now;
    the rest still needs per-target verdicts.
- **Collisions still produced usable evidence, under a different gene.** The RIG-I papers gave
  DDX58 six assay records (HTRF, BLI, SPR, MST, RNA pull-down, conformational quenching) and
  DHX36 two. Recording the protein actually assayed, rather than the target the paper was fetched
  for, is what keeps a collided batch from being a total loss.
- **Four targets with evidence have no protein class**, so nothing can link to them: AFF1, AKAP8,
  DDX58, IGFBP6. DDX58 is the notable one, with eight records and a live inhibitor and agonist
  literature.
- Four of the 20 targets never reached a paper: CALM2, LYPLA1, NTRK3 and PRKAA2. Eleven of the 60
  targets picked so far have none at all.
