# Paper crawl

The crawl reads open-access papers for one batch of targets at a time and logs two kinds of finding:

1. **Evidence that an assay type is used for a target.** One verified paper is enough. The crawl
   does not measure how often an assay is used.
2. **Technologies and assay types that our vocabulary lacks.** Each one is catalogued under a single
   proposed term, so the vocabulary can be extended from evidence.

Claude Code does the reading. `scripts/paper_crawl.py` does the bookkeeping. Nothing in the crawl
edits `data/`: findings reach the app only through a review chunk that the owner applies.

## Files

All crawl state is in `crawl/`. That folder is durable and is not regenerated by the build.

| file | one row per | written by |
|---|---|---|
| `targets.csv` | target picked, with batch, status and counts | `pick`, `worklist`, `log` |
| `papers.csv` | paper kept for a target | `worklist` |
| `worklists/batch_NN.csv` | methods block queued for reading | `worklist` |
| `skipped.csv` | block read and judged not an assay of the target, with the reason | `skip` |
| `evidence.csv` | assay record: target, functions, technologies, direction, matched entries, new links, new readouts, new technology, quote | `log` |
| `term_occurrences.csv` | vocabulary term a paper used that has no controlled value | `log` |
| `term_catalog.csv` | proposed new term, with the verbatim variants grouped under it | the agent, during the review pass |
| `alias_issues.csv` | alias that cost papers, with its kind and measured share | `alias`, enriched by `log` |
| `summaries/pass_NN.md` | periodic review pass | `summary`, plus the agent's observations |

The structured record for each paper is `build_output/papers/<pmcid>.json`, validated against
`schema/paper_extraction.schema.json`. `evidence.csv` carries the quote and PMCID, so the evidence
survives even if `build_output/` is cleared.

Target status in `targets.csv`:

- `picked`: drawn, not fetched.
- `fetched`: papers queued.
- `reading`: some blocks still open.
- `read`: every block has a record or a skip reason.
- `no_papers`: nothing passed the fetch gate.

A target in `targets.csv` is never picked again.

## The batch cycle

Run from the repository root.

```
py scripts\paper_crawl.py pick --n 20
py scripts\fetch_papers.py --genes <list printed by pick> --extract --backend claude
py scripts\paper_crawl.py worklist
```

`pick` draws 20 targets at random, stratified by protein class. It excludes every target already in
`targets.csv`. The pool is every protein with at least 20 assay-scoped Europe PMC hits and a subject
fraction of at least 0.7, so that the papers are about the protein. For each target, `fetch_papers.py`
keeps up to 10 papers that pass the assay-detail score. It queues only papers with a methods block
about the target, or whose title is about it.

Then read the batch:

```
[Console]::OutputEncoding = [Text.Encoding]::UTF8
py scripts\paper_crawl.py todo                 # blocks left per target
py scripts\paper_crawl.py triage PMC123        # section titles and flags of one paper
py scripts\paper_crawl.py show PMC123          # text of every open block of the paper
py scripts\paper_crawl.py show PMC123-A4 --max 6000
py scripts\paper_crawl.py skip "reason" PMC123-A1 PMC123-A2
py scripts\paper_crawl.py skip "reason" PMC123   # every remaining block of the paper
```

Set the console encoding first. Without it, PowerShell garbles µ, α and en dashes in the quotes.

Each block is flagged as follows:

- `subject`: the target is named as the thing assayed. Read these in full.
- `biomarker_only`: named, but as a marker or context. Usually skip.
- `no_mention`: the target is not named. Triage these by section title. A block called "Enzyme
  assay" in a paper about the target often says only "the enzyme". Read it.

For each block that is an assay of the target, write one lean JSON object to the block's `write_to`
path (see "Record format"). For each block that is not, log a skip with a short reason. When the
batch is finished:

```
py scripts\fetch_papers.py --agent-merge
py scripts\paper_crawl.py log
py scripts\paper_crawl.py summary
```

Then do the review pass (below).

## What counts as evidence

Record an assay when the block measures the target itself:

- its activity, binding or engagement;
- its activity or level in cells, when the paper ties the readout to the target, for example the
  product of the target enzyme, or a reporter for the target's pathway in a target-dependent design;
- a compound acting on it.

Do not record:

- **Phenotypes with the target as background.** Viability, migration, tumour growth, or Western
  blots of downstream proteins do not count, even when the paper is about the target.
- **Expression measurements.** Measuring the target's own mRNA or protein level (qPCR, Western,
  IHC) is not an assay of its function.
- **Another protein that shares a name or synonym.** Check the gene: name collisions cost about 6%
  of papers in batch 0. Examples are granzyme B "C11", GSR "GluR", and TRAIL versus "TRAIL-R2".
- **Buffer recipes, purification, cloning, docking and statistics blocks.**

One clear sentence that shows the target is assayed with a technique is enough. A detailed protocol
is not needed.

## Record format

These records are lean. They contain only the fields the crawl uses, and they validate against the
schema.

```json
{
  "assay_id": "PMC123-A4",
  "source_section": "2.5 Thallium flux assay",
  "assay_name_verbatim": "FluxOR thallium flux assay",
  "scope": "cell-based",
  "function_labels": ["Ion channel activity"],
  "technology_labels": [],
  "effect_direction": "activation",
  "pharmacology": ["positive_allosteric_modulator"],
  "target": {"gene_symbol": "KCNQ4", "species": "human"},
  "evidence": [{"field": "technology_labels", "quote": "<verbatim sentence>", "section": "2.5 Thallium flux assay"}],
  "confidence": "high",
  "unmapped_terms": [{"field": "technology_labels", "verbatim": "thallium flux (FluxOR) fluorescence assay",
                      "nearest_controlled_value": "Membrane potential dye", "quote": "<verbatim>"}]
}
```

Rules:

- **Vocabulary.** `function_labels` and `technology_labels` come only from `scripts/term_table.csv`.
  A technique with no term goes in `unmapped_terms` and is left out of `technology_labels`. It must
  not be forced into the nearest term.
- **Name a new technique consistently.** Before writing `verbatim`, check `crawl/term_catalog.csv`.
  If the technique is already catalogued, use one of its variants exactly, so that it is counted
  without hand matching.
- **Target gene.** `target.gene_symbol` is the protein assayed, which may differ from the target
  the paper was fetched for. A record for another protein is still logged.
- **Quotes.** The evidence quote must be verbatim and must name the technique or the target.
- **Confidence.** `high` means the block states the target and the technique. `medium` means one of
  the two is inferred from the same paper. `low` means it is plausible but not stated. Only high
  and medium count in the summaries.
- **Controlled enum values.** `scope` is `cell-free` or `cell-based`; there is no `biochemical`.
  `pharmacology` comes from the schema list: `agonist`, `partial_agonist`, `inverse_agonist`,
  `antagonist`, `positive_allosteric_modulator`, `negative_allosteric_modulator`,
  `allosteric_agonist`, `orthosteric_inhibitor`, `allosteric_inhibitor`, `covalent_inhibitor`,
  `degrader`, `stabiliser`, `direct_enzyme_activator`, `disruptor`, `unclear`. Plain `inhibitor`
  and `covalent` are not values. `record` does not check these; `--agent-merge` does, and a wrong
  value fails the whole paper. `effect_direction` is one of `inhibition`, `activation`, `both`,
  `binding_only`, `unclear` or omitted: `increase`, `decrease` and `no_effect` are not values
  (a degrader lowering target levels is `inhibition`; a glue driving complex formation is
  `activation`; a null result is `unclear`). `extraction_flags` is likewise a fixed list
  (`method_by_reference`, `values_from_figure`, `values_from_supplementary`, `kit_defaults_assumed`,
  `multiple_assays_merged_in_source`, `units_ambiguous`, `construct_range_uncertain`,
  `conflicting_values_in_paper`), not free text, and no other key may be added to a record.
  Validation only happens when `jsonschema` is importable, so install it into a scratchpad
  `--target` directory and set `PYTHONPATH` before merging; otherwise the merge prints
  "not validated" and reports every paper clean.
- **Direction.** `effect_direction` and `pharmacology` record what the paper states. Use
  `activation` only for an activator, agonist, opener or PAM. LPS used to switch on CASP4, for
  example, is not an activator.

## Name collisions

A target's search uses its gene symbol plus its UniProt synonyms. Some synonyms match something
else entirely, and the papers that come back are about another subject. This was 13% of papers in
batch 1. When reading shows a paper was lost this way, log it and move on:

```
py scripts\paper_crawl.py alias UNC119 RG4 --kind alias \
    --collides-with "RNA G-quadruplex (rG4)" --note "retinal protein 4"
```

`--kind` is one of:

- `alias`: a synonym that means something else (UNC119 "RG4" vs RNA G-quadruplex "rG4").
- `species`: the same symbol in another organism (INHA vs *M. tuberculosis* InhA).
- `family`: a relative or binding partner (TNFSF10 "TRAIL" vs its receptor TRAIL-R2).
- `mutation_notation`: a point-mutation label (F13A1 "F13A" vs apelin-13(F13A)).

`log` then fills `papers_lost` and the measured share: the fraction of the target's cached hits that
name only this alias and neither the gene symbol nor the full protein name.

**The aliases are not changed as they are found, for two reasons.**

1. A single rule cannot decide them. 1,381 of the 3,149 eligible targets carry a short synonym, and
   for many it is the name the literature actually uses: dropping short synonyms would lose TGR5 for
   GPBAR1 and GCN2 for EIF2AK4. The measured share separates only the extreme case (UNC119 0.92); a
   real collision and a legitimate common name score alike in the middle (F13A1 0.42, GPBAR1 0.48).
   So the verdict is per target, and a useful rule needs more verdicts than two batches give.
2. Changing an alias invalidates the cached Europe PMC search for every target already crawled, so
   the searches would have to be re-run with `--refresh`. Doing that once, after several batches,
   costs one pass instead of one per batch.

So collect verdicts while crawling, and apply them in a single pass when the file is large enough to
generalise from. Keep `action` as `open` until then; set it to `applied` or `kept` when it is
decided, with the reason in `note`.

## The review pass

Run a review pass after every batch:

1. Run `py scripts\paper_crawl.py summary`. It writes `crawl/summaries/pass_NN.md` with three
   generated sections:
   - **Section 1** lists the vocabulary: each catalogued term with its paper and target counts,
     then every verbatim term not yet catalogued.
   - **Section 2** lists new evidence from this pass. It shows only rows the app does not already
     show: an entry link the target lacks today, a readout no campaign states, or a technology
     with no term.
   - **Section 3** shows the outcome for each target read in the pass.
2. Catalogue the terms. For each uncatalogued verbatim term, either add it to the `variants` of an
   existing row in `crawl/term_catalog.csv` (pipe-separated, lower case, exact text), or add a new
   row. Re-run `log` and `summary` until nothing is left uncatalogued.
   A term's `kind` (`technology`, `function`, `system`, `counter_screen`) lives **only** in
   `term_catalog.csv`. Records validate against `schema/paper_extraction.schema.json`, which has no
   `kind` at all, and `term_occurrences.csv` carries `field`, not `kind`. So renaming or merging a
   `kind` later is a one-column edit over 88 rows — it does not invalidate any record and does not
   mean re-reading anything. Do not hold up a batch waiting for a `kind` to be settled.
3. Set the status of each term:
   - `candidate`: 1 paper.
   - `supported`: 2 or more papers.
   - `proposed`: in a review chunk.
   - `added`: in the term table.
   - `rejected`: with the reason in `notes`.
4. Write section 4, "Observations", in a few lines:
   - terms ready to propose;
   - repeated link patterns (for example, cellular activity entry ENZ3 for metabolic enzymes);
   - targets with no protein class;
   - name collisions;
   - anything systemic.

   Keep it at the level of patterns, not individual papers.

Findings go to the app only when the owner asks for them. Supported terms become a term-table
review chunk, and new entry links become a links chunk, in `review/NN_*`. Each chunk is grouped by
decision, so one decision covers a whole group. Adding a technology term triggers a relabel of the
campaigns, so terms are proposed together, not one at a time.

## Prompt to continue the crawl

Paste this into Claude Code from the repository root:

> Continue the paper crawl described in docs/PAPER_CRAWL.md. If `crawl/targets.csv` has a batch
> whose targets are `fetched` or `reading`, finish reading it. Otherwise pick the next batch of 20
> with `paper_crawl.py pick`, then fetch it and build the worklist. Read every block, following the
> evidence and record rules in the doc. Write one lean record per assay of the target, and a skip
> with a reason for every other block. Use catalogued term variants for new techniques. When the
> batch is read, merge, run `log` and `summary`, catalogue every new term, and write the
> observations for the pass. Log every name collision with `paper_crawl.py alias` as you hit it,
> but do not change any alias. Do not edit `data/` and do not create review chunks unless I ask.
> Report in Standard Technical English, at the level of patterns:
> - the new terms and their paper counts;
> - targets that gained an entry link or a readout;
> - targets with no class;
> - any systemic issue.

  papers.
  - New terms (supported): manual patch clamp, thallium flux, MST, HPLC/HPTLC, coupled-enzyme
    metabolite detection, flow cytometry.
  - Most common new link: ENZ3 cellular activity for metabolic enzymes (ACLY, GLO1, SRD5A1, GSR,
    MGMT).
  - RGS4 has no protein class.
  - See `crawl/summaries/pass_01.md`.
- **Batch 1 (2026-11-16/17).** 20 targets, 65 papers, 395 blocks, 36 records.
  - New assay class with no entry at all: phase separation (droplet imaging, FRAP, opto-droplets).
  - New terms: GloSensor, FRAP, phase separation, genetically encoded biosensor, TR-FIA, in vitro
    motility, pyrene-actin, chemoproteomic pull-down, thromboelastometry.
  - Name collisions cost 19 of 145 papers (13%), including all 10 for UNC119 ("RG4" vs "rG4") and
    8 of 10 for INHA (*M. tuberculosis* InhA).
  - See `crawl/summaries/pass_02.md`.
- **Batch 2 (2026-11-17).** 20 targets, 75 papers, 515 blocks, 100 records.
  - The vocabulary has no RNA-binding or chromatin terms at all: RNA pull-down, RIP, eCLIP,
    ChIP-qPCR and the pre-mRNA splicing function were all new. A whole protein class is unlabelled.
  - Enzyme function terms stop at the generic "Enzyme activity": no glycosyltransferase (OGT) and
    no desaturase (FADS2) term exists.
  - Target engagement outgrew CETSA: PISA and ITDRF both appeared in one paper.
  - Worst collisions yet: all 10 RPS15 papers were RIG-I ("RIG"), and 6 of 10 AFF1 papers matched
    "AF4", the abbreviation for asymmetrical flow field-flow fractionation. The collided papers
    still gave DDX58 eight records, logged under the gene actually assayed.
  - 41 new terms catalogued; ENZ3 cellular activity was again the most common new link.
  - See `crawl/summaries/pass_03.md`.
- **Batch 3 (2026-11-18).** The first chosen batch rather than a random draw: the 20 proteins with
  the most campaigns in `usage_stats.csv`, picked with the new `pick --genes`. 133 papers, 867
  blocks, 160 records — the biggest batch so far, and the least productive per paper.
  - Not one primary activity or binding readout was missing from the vocabulary. For a
    well-covered target the papers confirm the vocabulary rather than extend it, and new terms per
    paper fell by about half against batch 2.
  - What they do add sits around the assay: the biological system it runs in (Ba/F3, mono-CYP line,
    HepaRG, liver organoid, donor microsome panel, ex vivo PDX) and the counter-screens every
    campaign runs (Ames, CiPA, cellular kinome panel, CYP phenotyping, knockdown control). Two new
    catalogue `kind` values, `system` and `counter_screen`, hold these; the owner should accept or
    rename them.
  - Target engagement is now six methods under one CETSA term: DARTS (x2), TPP, CFAST, plus PISA
    and ITDRF from batch 2. The clearest term-splitting job outstanding.
  - The one missing function is the disease-relevant one: KDR's angiogenic sprouting assays (tube
    formation, spheroid sprouting, aortic ring, CAM) had no term. Catalogued as F010, supported.
  - KCNH2 has the most campaigns of any protein in the database and queued no paper at all; its
    only evidence comes from being the hERG counter-screen inside other targets' papers. Safety
    anti-targets are assayed constantly and published never.
  - Collisions: MET lost 3 of 6 papers (methionine, MetRS, metformin) and SRC pulled in steroid
    receptor coactivator papers. 40 papers lost across the crawl. The collided opioid paper still
    gave OPRD1 five records.
  - A paper can now be queued under several genes of one batch, which broke block ids in five
    papers; `worklist` gives every distinct block a single shared id.
  - 37 new terms catalogued. See `crawl/summaries/pass_04.md`.
